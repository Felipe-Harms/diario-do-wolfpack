# Privacy policy and red lines

> What does **not** belong in this repository.

This repository is public. The content is the first narrative layer of the Wolfpack — written to be read by anyone. For that reason, there is an explicit list of what stays out.

## What does not belong

**About people and routine.**

- Nothing of Felipe's personal routine or of anyone involved in the project.
- Nothing about schedule, commute, personal data, family, health, leisure.

**About clients and third parties.**

- Nothing about clients, cases, proceedings, private projects, names of external companies.
- Nothing about third-party content without explicit authorization.

**About secrets and infrastructure.**

- Nothing about credentials, tokens, keys, passwords, internal paths, internal IPs, hosts, ports, private domains.
- Nothing about UUIDs or internal IDs of any subsystem.
- Nothing about panel screenshots, dashboards, logs, alerts, operational metrics.
- Nothing that reveals exploitable topology — this repository must not let a reader discover how the system is assembled, where it lives, or how to attack it.

**About private content.**

- Nothing about internal memories, sessions, runs, transcripts, or proofs generated by tools.
- Nothing about private communications with Felipe or between components of the Wolfpack.
- Nothing about decisions that depend on non-public information.

## What **does** belong, and how

- Facts already public, or already made public by Felipe on another surface.
- Narrative milestones reconstructed from what is public, without exposing what is private.
- Analysis of decisions and protocols, when those are themselves the object of the narrative.
- Items without verifiable public proof, **provided they are explicitly declared** as decision or retrospective account — never presented as verifiable fact.

Before publishing, three questions:

1. If Felipe read this, would he agree it is fair to publish?
2. If a third party read this, would they gain new information about infrastructure, operations, or routine?
3. Reading this again a year from now, does the tone hold?

If the answer is "no" on any of them, the entry does not go into the repository.

## How review works

Every future entry goes through branch + PR. Five entries pass through mandatory human review before any automation. No cron, no hook, no GitHub Actions automating publication. The flow is manual and auditable.
